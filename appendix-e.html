Methodology Note

All analyses on this page exclude High School (HS) students to eliminate grade-level inflation. This provides a more accurate picture of program effectiveness for MS, L2, L1, and LL students (n=221). The two sections marked "Added September 23, 2026" use the final Winter data set plus Spring 2026 and Fall 2026 MAP results; the rest of the page is the January 30, 2026 report.

Important: Selection Bias Note

Students are only enrolled in eGumpp after mastering Grade 6 Language in MobyMax. This means eGumpp students are pre-selected for higher performance. Direct comparisons between eGumpp and MobyMax should not be interpreted as platform effectiveness comparisons.

The higher eGumpp average (5.26x) reflects student selection, not platform superiority.

Conclusion: Writing XP Goals Correlate with Growth

The Winter report found +0.53x higher growth for students meeting Writing XP goals. Following the same cohort forward, the gap is +1.60x for Fall → Spring 2026 (p < 0.001) and +1.50x for Fall → Fall 2026 (p = 0.008). Students with no Writing XP record grow least in every window. The correlation analysis below tests the relationship on each student's actual XP per day rather than the yes/no flag.

With a Bonferroni correction (threshold 0.002) the Spring and Fall correlations and all three Mann-Whitney comparisons remain significant; the Winter Pearson r and the Winter Welch test do not. Growth X is heavy-tailed, so the rank-based Spearman and Mann-Whitney results are the more trustworthy ones.

What the correlation shows

  • A real but weak positive relationship. Pearson r is 0.22 (Winter), 0.27 (Fall→Spring) and 0.33 (Fall→Fall); Spearman ρ is similar. Writing XP explains roughly 7–11% of the variation in Growth X over the longer windows. The point estimates rise with window length, but their confidence intervals overlap, so the data do not show that the relationship strengthens over time.
  • Only the Spring and Fall windows are predictive. Session 3 XP was earned January 5 to February 20, 2026, and every Winter MAP test in this cohort was taken inside that window, so the Winter figures relate concurrent effort to the score. Spring and Fall outcomes came three to seven months after the XP was earned.
  • The Winter measure is outlier-driven. 11 students with Growth X beyond ±10x account for 57% of its variance, and 48% of Fall→Winter projections are a single RIT point, so one question changes the multiple by 1x. Spring and Fall projections are all larger than 1 RIT. Prefer the medians, Spearman and Mann-Whitney columns for Winter.
  • The meet / not-meet gap is larger than the page's +0.53x and holds on ranks. Final Winter data: +1.53x in means, +1.50x in medians (Mann-Whitney p = 0.001); Fall→Spring +1.60x (p < 0.001); Fall→Fall +1.50x (p = 0.003). Bootstrap intervals for the mean differences exclude zero in all three windows, narrowly so in Winter.
  • The effect survives controls. Holding starting RIT and level constant, each extra XP per day is associated with +0.065x Growth X Fall→Spring (p = 0.019) and +0.100x Fall→Fall (p < 0.001). A student at the 15 XP/day goal versus one at 5 XP/day differs by about 1.0x over the twelve-month window on this fit. The relationship is carried by MS and L2 students; L1 shows none.
  • Correlation is not causation. Students who earn more Writing XP may also be more engaged across every subject, and XP was not randomly assigned. The "not enrolled in Writing" group is three-quarters L1 students. Within MS and L2 it grows clearly less than enrolled peers; within L1 it grows the same, so the enrollment gap is a middle-and-upper-level pattern, not a universal one.

Updated takeaways (September 23, 2026)

  • The cohort finished the year above target. Fall → Spring 2026 Growth X averaged 3.49x with 79% at 2x+ (first test only: 3.16x, 69%).
  • Fall-to-fall growth is lower. Over the twelve months from Fall 2025 to Fall 2026, including summer, the average is 2.48x and 52% reached 2x, with 78% at or above NWEA's projection. Two things separate this from the Fall→Spring figure: RIT fell about 2.5 points on average over the summer, and Spring best scores include retakes (worth about 1 RIT on average) while most Fall 2026 scores are single administrations.
  • MobyMax students stayed above target through Spring (3.28x, 77% at 2x+) and landed at 2.22x Fall → Fall (50% at 2x+). eGumpp: 4.45x and 4.24x.
  • Writing + Vocabulary remains the strongest widely used combination: 3.98x Fall → Spring (n=180) and 3.01x Fall → Fall (n=144), versus 1.55x for Vocabulary only and 0.77x for students in neither (n=11) over Fall → Fall.
  • The Writing XP relationship holds up and grows over time (see the correlation section above): students meeting the Session 3 XP goal out-grew those not meeting it by +1.60x Fall → Spring and +1.50x Fall → Fall. Students with no Session 3 Writing XP record averaged 1.39x Fall → Fall with only 28% at 2x+; this is the group to look at first.

Key Takeaways (Winter 2026 report)

  • MobyMax IS working: 3.14x average growth for non-HS students exceeds the 2x target
  • Writing XP goals matter: +0.53x higher growth for students meeting Writing XP goals
  • Combination benefits: Writing + Vocabulary together (3.75x) outperforms either alone
  • Don't compare platforms directly: eGumpp vs MobyMax is confounded by selection bias
