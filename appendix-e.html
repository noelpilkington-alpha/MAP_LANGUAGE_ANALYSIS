Methodology Note

All analyses on this page exclude High School (HS) students to eliminate grade-level inflation. This provides a more accurate picture of program effectiveness for MS, L2, L1, and LL students (n=221). The two sections marked "Added September 23, 2026" use the final Winter data set plus Spring 2026 and Fall 2026 MAP results; the rest of the page is the January 30, 2026 report.

Important: Selection Bias Note

Students are only enrolled in eGumpp after mastering Grade 6 Language in MobyMax. This means eGumpp students are pre-selected for higher performance. Direct comparisons between eGumpp and MobyMax should not be interpreted as platform effectiveness comparisons.

The higher eGumpp average (5.26x) reflects student selection, not platform superiority.

What the correlation shows

  • A real but weak-to-moderate positive relationship. In the Winter 2026 data, r = 0.22 (p = 0.002), ρ = 0.28. Writing XP explains about 5% of the variation in Growth X; most of the variation comes from other factors.
  • The relationship strengthens over longer windows. r rises from 0.22 (Winter) to 0.27 (Fall→Spring) to 0.33 (Fall→Fall), all significant. Winter→Spring alone shows no relationship (r = 0.05); that window has a projected growth of only 1–2 RIT points, so the multiples are mostly noise.
  • The meet / not-meet gap is larger than the page's +0.53x. With the final Winter data it is +1.53x (p = 0.043); for Fall→Spring it is +1.60x (p < 0.001); for Fall→Fall it is +1.50x (p = 0.008).
  • Slope: each extra XP per day is associated with roughly +0.11x Growth X in Winter and +0.10x over Fall→Fall. A student at the 15 XP/day goal versus one at 5 XP/day differs by about 1.0x over a full year on this fit.
  • Correlation is not causation. Students who earn more Writing XP may also be more engaged across every subject. The "no Writing XP data" group (not enrolled in Writing in Session 3) grows least in every window, which points to enrollment and engagement rather than XP itself.

Updated takeaways (September 23, 2026)

  • The cohort finished the year above target. Fall → Spring 2026 Growth X averaged 3.49x with 79% at 2x+ (first test only: 3.16x, 69%).
  • Fall-to-fall growth is lower. Over the twelve months from Fall 2025 to Fall 2026, including summer, the average is 2.48x and 52% reached 2x, with 78% at or above NWEA's projection. Part of this is summer loss and part is regression to the mean after the retake-boosted Winter and Spring scores.
  • MobyMax students stayed above target through Spring (3.28x, 77% at 2x+) and landed at 2.22x Fall → Fall (50% at 2x+). eGumpp: 4.45x and 4.24x.
  • Writing + Vocabulary remains the strongest widely used combination: 3.98x Fall → Spring (n=180) and 3.01x Fall → Fall (n=144), versus 1.55x for Vocabulary only and 0.77x for students in neither (n=11) over Fall → Fall.
  • The Writing XP relationship holds up and grows over time (see the correlation section above): students meeting the Session 3 XP goal out-grew those not meeting it by +1.60x Fall → Spring and +1.50x Fall → Fall. Students with no Session 3 Writing XP record averaged 1.39x Fall → Fall with only 28% at 2x+; this is the group to look at first.

Key Takeaways (Winter 2026 report)

  • MobyMax IS working: 3.14x average growth for non-HS students exceeds the 2x target
  • Writing XP goals matter: +0.53x higher growth for students meeting Writing XP goals
  • Combination benefits: Writing + Vocabulary together (3.75x) outperforms either alone
  • Don't compare platforms directly: eGumpp vs MobyMax is confounded by selection bias
  • Deep dive needed: 15 students enrolled in Writing and meeting XP goals but not growing - see Deep Dive page
